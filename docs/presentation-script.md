# Tabiato — 5-Minute Presentation Script

**Record from:** Figma Presentation mode (▶ top-right), full screen, screen recording + mic.
**Hard limit:** 5:00. Going over loses Presentation marks.
**Do not cover:** the ideas you dropped, the mentor feedback, the ideation boards. Ideation (25%)
is scored from the README only — spending video time on it costs you twice.

**Spend the five minutes on four things:** what it is, why it is novel, how it will be built, why
it deserves to be built.

---

```
05 Home → 15 Add sheet → 17 Select photos → 18 Details → 19 Added
```

## The script

### 0:00 – 0:25 · Hook
**Screen: `05 Home — Memory Map`, still. Do not click yet.**

> Four people go on the same trip and come home with four separate camera rolls.
>
> Two months later nobody remembers what that noodle place was called, who paid for what, or
> which day was the good one. The trip happened. The record of it didn't.
>
> This is Tabiato. It's where a trip goes after it ends.

---

### 0:25 – 0:50 · Positioning
**Screen: still on Home. Trace the pins with the cursor.**

> Open the app and you don't see "where do you want to go". You see where you've already been.
> Every trip you've recorded is a pin, and the number on it is how many memories are inside.
>
> The planner is this small button in the corner. That placement is the whole argument:
> **memory first, and planning falls out of it.**

---

### 0:50 – 1:50 · The core — recording
**Click: ＋ → `15` → `17` → `18` → `19`**

> Recording is where every travel journal app dies, because nobody wants to type after a day of
> walking. So we ask for exactly one thing: your photos.
>
> *(on 17)* Ato tells you what it can do before you do any work — "12 photos have location data,
> I can pin them to 3 places automatically."
>
> *(on 18)* The place is already detected from the photo's GPS. Spend, rating and notes are
> optional. You can dump twenty photos while standing in a queue and fill the rest in on the
> train home.
>
> And if a photo has no location data, we **ask** — we never guess. A wrong pin puts the wrong
> day in the wrong place and quietly breaks the whole story.
>
> *(on 19)* One photo selection: eight memories, three places, RM 195 logged.

---

### 1:50 – 2:20 · The unit is a place
**Screen: `10 Location Detail`. Scroll slowly down the whole screen.**

> This one screen is the entire product.
>
> We don't store a photo. We store **a place you went** — these eight shots, what it cost, the
> rating you gave it, your note, and what the people who were with you said, right here.
>
> A shared album gives you the first one. It's the other four that make everything after this
> possible.

---

### 2:20 – 2:55 · Shared trips
**Click: `21 Trip members` → back to `08b`**

> This is the part we care most about.
>
> When Max uploads his photos of the same lunch, they don't become a second album. They land on
> the **same place record** as yours — next to the same bill, the same rating.
>
> Four camera rolls become one trip. And it belongs to all of you: any member can open it a year
> later and find the whole thing intact, including the parts they never photographed.

---

### 2:55 – 3:45 · The AI reads it back
**Click: `32b` → `25b` → `26` → `27`. Pause on the book page.**

> When the trip ends, Ato reads everything you recorded and writes it into a book.
>
> *(on 27 — point at the line, and hold here)*
>
> **"Every stop you rated 5 stars was one you arrived at before 9am."**
>
> Nobody told the app that. It read the ratings and the timestamps on the place records and found
> the pattern itself.
>
> A photo book can lay your pictures out beautifully. It can't tell you something true about
> yourself that you hadn't noticed.
>
> *(click 14 Travel Insights)* After a few trips this becomes your profile — your real pace, where
> your money actually goes, what you consistently rate highest.

---

### 3:45 – 4:05 · Search
**Click: `12` → `13`**

> "What was that noodle place called?" now has an answer. You ask in plain language, and it
> searches your own memories.

---

### 4:05 – 4:35 · The planner, last
**Click: `28` → `35` → `31` → `33`**

> Only now does planning show up — because by this point the app knows who you are.
>
> Most AI planners **recommend** places to you. We do the opposite. You list the places you'd be
> annoyed to miss, and Ato promises: *"I'll fit the rest around these three. Nothing you added
> gets moved or dropped."*
>
> *(on 31, point at the tag)* Your stops are marked **yours**. The AI does ordering, timing and
> gap-filling — the part humans are genuinely bad at.
>
> *(on 33)* And alerts aren't weather reports. "Day four, these two stops are affected — swap with
> day five, it's all indoors."

---

### 4:35 – 5:00 · Close
**Screen: `23 Community`, or zoom out to the full Figma board.**

> Next.js, Supabase, MapLibre, free-tier AI. Zero cost to run, buildable in a month — core
> features on a real database, community front-end first.
>
> There are apps that remember your photos but understand nothing. There are apps that understand
> planning but remember nothing.
>
> **Tabiato is the gap between them** — it turns what actually happened into structured memory,
> and then uses it to write your next trip.

---

## Before you record

1. **Time a dry run.** Over 5:00 costs marks directly. If you're long, cut sentences from 3:45–4:05
   and 4:35–5:00 first — never cut the book page at 2:55–3:45.
2. **Hold on the book page.** That single line is the strongest moment in the video. Give the
   viewer three full seconds to read it.
3. **Move the cursor deliberately.** Point at the thing you're describing. Judges follow the
   cursor.
4. **Say "Ato", not "the AI".** Naming it makes the product feel finished.

## If you have a spare 15 seconds

Add after the Community beat, before the close:

> Everything you share is per-field — photos, spend and comments toggle separately, and your
> private notes stay private by default.

That closes the privacy question before a judge has to ask it.
